My research connects population health questions with statistical analysis, field research, and communication. Much of my work has focused on health in Mongolia, alongside research using U.S. population data.

## Hypertension, diabetes, and primary care

At the Onom Foundation, I have worked on population-based studies of hypertension and its risk factors, as well as research on diabetes awareness, treatment, and control.

Through the **Better Hearts Better Cities** initiative, I helped develop and lead field research for a representative survey of 4,515 adults in Ulaanbaatar and carried out statistical analysis. I also contributed to studies of primary care professionals' knowledge, attitudes, and practices in hypertension management.

Related publications:

- [Hypertension prevalence and control in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia](https://doi.org/10.1111/jch.13784)
- [Diabetes management cascade in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0337413)
- [Hypertension knowledge, attitudes, and practices of nurses and physicians](https://doi.org/10.1111/jch.13592)

## Air pollution and cardiovascular health

My research interests include the spatial relationship between air pollution and hypertension in Ulaanbaatar, and the use of supervised machine learning to study environmental exposures and cardiovascular health.

A related conference abstract, **Spatial association between hypertension and air pollution in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia**, appeared in the Journal of Hypertension in 2024.

## Liver disease and disease burden estimation

As a researcher at **IHME, University of Washington**, I worked with the Reproductive, Genitourinary, and Digestive Diseases team on liver disease and cirrhosis. My responsibilities included assessing data quality, extracting data for disease modeling, and preparing visual summaries.

At Mongolia's **Center for Health Development**, I led disease burden estimation work and contributed statistical analysis to national health research.

My research interests also include viral hepatitis and the use of unsupervised machine learning to explore the diagnosis and classification of hepatitis D virus infection.

## Acculturation, nutrition, and health equity

My MPH thesis examined the relationship between acculturation and dietary sodium-to-potassium ratios, and differences in trends by race and ethnicity, using **NHANES 2011-2020** data.

The project used complex survey methods, trend analysis, and multivariable regression to study relationships between dietary patterns and social and demographic characteristics.

[Read the MPH thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/bitstreams/b104ff3c-665a-43ad-ab60-926328e8013d/download).

## Methods and tools

My analytical experience includes complex survey analysis, longitudinal and trend analysis, missing data analysis, spatial analysis and GIS, data visualization, and applications of machine learning.

I work primarily with **R** and **SPSS**, with additional experience in **ArcGIS/ArcMap**, **Epi Info**, and foundational use of **Python**, **SQL**, **SAS**, **Stata**, **Octave**, **Git**, and high-performance computing.
